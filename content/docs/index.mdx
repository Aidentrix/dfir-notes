This site documents my study of digital forensics and incident response through hands-on exercises, public training datasets, and direct examination of forensic artifacts.

The working method is deliberately evidence-first:

> **Artifact → structure → raw evidence → interpretation → validation**

## What this site covers

The current focus is filesystem forensics, Windows artifacts, Registry analysis, timeline reasoning, and reproducible laboratory work.

## Why the low-level details matter

Forensic tools are useful, but the goal is not to memorize tool output. The goal is to understand enough of the underlying structure to answer:

- What artifact produced this result?
- Where does that artifact live?
- What field or byte sequence supports the conclusion?
- How can the result be independently validated?

## Training scope

Laboratory exercises use public or otherwise authorized training data. They are educational analyses and are not presented as real-world investigative casework.
