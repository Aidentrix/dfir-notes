---
title: PICL Principles
description: Carrier's preservation, isolation, correlation, and logging guidelines for developing forensic procedures.
---

PICL is Carrier's compact set of guidelines for performing and documenting forensic procedures:

~~~text
P  Preservation
I  Isolation
C  Correlation
L  Logging
~~~

## Source basis

The framework comes from the **General Guidelines** section of Chapter 1 of Brian Carrier's *File System Forensic Analysis*.

It is guidance for forensic procedure, not a substitute for an organization's legal, laboratory, regulatory, or chain-of-custody requirements.

## Preservation

Avoid unnecessary modification of potential evidence.

Carrier's examples include analyzing copies, calculating hashes of important data, using write-blocking controls where appropriate, and minimizing actions that may overwrite evidence.

The practical question is:

> What could this examination step change, and how am I controlling or documenting that risk?

## Isolation

Keep the analysis environment appropriately separated from suspect data and from outside systems.

Carrier emphasizes that suspect content can contain executable or active material and that live analysis inherently weakens isolation because the examiner is operating through the suspect system itself.

## Correlation

Support important findings with independent evidence or observations when practical.

For this learning path, correlation also means comparing manual structure decoding with a second representation such as `fdisk`, `fsstat`, another parser, or another artifact.

## Logging

Document actions, searches, commands, results, and procedural decisions.

Good logging lets another examiner understand not merely the final value, but how it was obtained.

~~~text
command
→ input object
→ relevant output
→ interpretation
→ validation
→ conclusion
~~~

PICL is therefore visible throughout both the Fundamentals pages and the CFReDS lab.
